Lighting someone's piece in ShowXpress

You've been handed a song and a list of cues, in whatever shape they wrote it. This is how to turn that into a show the venue's lights play, from what we learned doing the Teen Titans showcase. Everything up to the last section needs no programming.

Where the facts come from

ShowXpress is also sold as The Lighting Controller; it's the same program. Menu names here come from the V9 manual (in the manual folder of the ShowXpress download, cited as "V9 p12") and from what we saw on a Mac running 9.2.6. A few things neither the manual nor our testing settled; those are marked Not confirmed. The older V6 manual has a different layout, so don't follow it step by step.

Words Everyone

Fixture
One light. Ours were 13 SlimPAR 56s, LED pars that mix any colour.
Channel
One thing a fixture lets you set, as a number from 0 to 255. Our SlimPARs have 7: red, green, blue, colour macro, shutter, mode, dimmer. Colour is the red/green/blue mix; brightness is the dimmer.
DMX, address, patch
DMX is the signal on the cable. Each fixture listens from its address onward (ours: 1, 33, 65 …). The patch is the list of which fixture is at which address. The venue sets it; you copy it.
Scene and step
A scene is a saved look, or a sequence of looks. Each step in it sets every channel and has a duration. Each channel either snaps or fades into the step, and a fade takes the step's whole duration (V9 p1, p13).
TimeLine
Scenes laid out under the music, so the show plays itself (V9 p25).
Buttons
Scenes on a page of buttons, for someone to press on cue (V9 p20).
Cue
One change at one moment: "at 0:27.5, all lights fade to warm white over 2 seconds."

Before you start Everyone

Ask the venue tech

  • Does the show run from a laptop with ShowXpress, or does the tech run it on their own desk? If theirs, they may only want a cue sheet.
  • Can they send the ShowXpress show, or at least its fixtures.ini and fixture files? That saves patching by hand and guarantees the addresses match.
  • Which light on the stage map is which fixture in the patch? Ask about any light in the patch that's not on the map.
  • Which DMX adapter do they use, and will it be plugged into the laptop at the show?
  • Are strobes and fast flashing allowed? Does a warning need posting?

Get from the performer

  • The exact music file the show will play. Every time is measured against that file. A re-export that starts a little later shifts every cue.
  • Their cue list, however it's written.
  • A video of them doing the piece, if they have one. It shows what needs lighting.

Turn their cue list into times Everyone

Cue lists arrive as lyrics ("blue when she sings 'falling'"), counts ("8 counts into the chorus"), bars, rough times, or a feeling. Whatever the shape, rewrite it as a table before you touch ShowXpress:

TimeLightsColourLevelHowUntil
0:00.00alldark0—
0:04.321–12lime / lavender100%snapnext cue
0:27.54allwarm white100%fade 2 s
0:57.667white100%snap on, fade out 0.3 s, ×40:58.26

Finding the times

  • Play the song in anything that shows the time to a tenth or better (an audio editor, or this site's lighting page). A hit lit 0.2 s late looks late.
  • For counts, find the first count of the phrase, then the beat length: 60 ÷ BPM seconds. At 120 BPM a count is 0.5 s, and an "&" lands halfway between.
  • If a figure repeats, time it once and copy it. The repeat is usually the same distance apart.

Ask the performer when it's unclear

These are where we got it wrong the first time:

  • "Fade": fade in, fade out, or blend from one colour to another? How long?
  • When does it stop? "Chaos in the last section" turned out to mean two separate bursts.
  • Which lights? "Light her up" might mean her apparatus at full and everything else dimmed.
  • Read the rhythm back to them ("1, and 2, &?"). People mis-say counts; we lost a round to it.

Then show them something (a recording of the 3D view, below) as early as you can. Most of our big changes came from the performer seeing it.

1. Set up the show Everyone

  1. Install ShowXpress (the ShowXpress guide covers the Mac install).
  2. File › New lightshow, named after the piece (V9 p4).
  3. Shows live in your home folder at ~/TheLightingController/LightShows/<show>; File › Open lightshow Folder opens it. Not the lightshows_bank folder next to the app: files put there are ignored.

If the venue sent their files

Quit ShowXpress (Cmd + Q). Put their fixtures.ini in the show folder and their fixture files in its fixtures/ folder. Reopen; Editor › Fixtures should list every light.

If not, patch by hand

  1. Editor › Fixtures, then Add fixtures (or right-click the grid › Add fixture) (V9 p7–8).
  2. Find the fixture model in the bank, in the mode the venue uses (ours: 7CH). Missing? Import a fixture from a file the venue or manufacturer gives you (V9 p8).
  3. Type the starting address and how many, then Patch. For lights that aren't evenly spaced, patch them one at a time.
  4. Save the dmx addressing (V9 p7). Rename each fixture to its stage map number (right-click › Rename fixture). It saves confusion later.

2. Make the looks Everyone

Go to Editor › Steps. Each fixture gets a row of faders, one per channel (V9 p12–13).

One look

  1. New scene.
  2. For each light, set red, green and blue for the colour and dimmer for brightness. Click a value to type it. Leave the other channels at 0: on ours, a non-zero shutter or mode turns on the fixture's built-in strobe and effects.
  3. Check the small fade / snap icon under each fader. Right-click a fader › Set "fade" state of channel on all steps does a whole column at once.
  4. Type the step's Duration in the step list. It's minutes:seconds.hundredths, so 00:02.00 is two seconds.
  5. Save scene as. Name it so it sorts in show order: 03 warm white fade.

Set every light in every scene, even the dark ones (dimmer 0). A scene that leaves a light out lets whatever was there before carry through.

Fades

A fade takes the whole step, so a 2-second fade is a 2-second step with its channels on fade. To fade and then stay, add a second step with the same values on snap, held as long as the look lasts.

Multi-step scenes loop (V9 p26, "Loop"). If the last step is too short, the scene starts over and runs its fade again. We hit this when a hold step meant to last minutes lasted 6 seconds.

Hits, pulses and strobes

  • Hit: step 1 snaps on, held for the hit (say 0.05 s); step 2 fades to dark over 0.3 s.
  • Pulse or breathing: steps alternate full and about 35%, all on fade, each a beat long.
  • Strobe: steps alternate on and off, on snap. ShowXpress plays steps in 0.05 s ticks and rounds each one up (we measured it: 40 steps of 0.07 s took 3.97 s, not 2.8 s). Make every step a multiple of 0.05 s, or fast runs drift off the beat.
  • Ripples and chases: Editor › Generator draws a curve per channel with a delay between fixtures (V9 p15–16). Or make the steps by hand with each light a little later.

Flashing more than 3 times a second is the usual photosensitivity line (WCAG 2.3.1 is the web version). Check the venue's rules.

Looks that worked for us

  • Solos: the soloist's apparatus warm white at full, the other dancer's dark, everything else about 55% in soft colours.
  • In busy sections, keep the dancer's light at full and the rest lower (we used 50%), so they don't disappear.
  • Snaps for punchy intros. Hits that snap on and fade away. Crossfades for calm passages.
  • Pick a palette (ours was the five logo colours) and stay in it.

3. Put it on the song Everyone

Use the TimeLine when the show should play itself with the music. Go to Live › TimeLine.

  1. Menu › TimeLine settings: set the duration longer than the song, and turn on Media timelines so there's a row for the music (V9 p25, p27).
  2. Right-click the music row › Add Media file, and pick the song. Use a WAV to see the waveform; it only draws for WAV (V9 p25, p27). On a Mac, afconvert -f WAVE -d LEI16 song.mp3 song.wav in Terminal converts one.
  3. Right-click a light row › Add Steps scene, and pick a scene (V9 p25).
  4. Right-click the block › Move to › specific time, and type the cue time (V9 p26). Dragging isn't precise enough. The timeline works in tenths of a second.
  5. Make each block last until the next one starts.
  6. Save the timeline (V9 p25).

To play: Play runs from the start. To start partway, double-click the top of the timeline to put the red marker there, then Play from red marker (V9 p25–26). Keypad +/− zoom, PgUp/PgDn scroll.

Not confirmed: how to set a block's length in the app (dragging its edge is my best guess; the manual doesn't say). What happens when two rows set the same light at once. Whether lights go dark in a gap between blocks. We kept to one light row, blocks end to end, every scene setting every light, which sidesteps all three.

We saw lights blink at the joins between blocks, especially near snaps and short fades. What fixed it: joins inside a held look, at least a quarter second from any snap, with each scene starting by snapping to the exact look at that moment. Longer blocks (several looks each) blink less than one block per look.

Or: press GO by hand Everyone

For a tech who wants to cue live, or as a backup, put scenes on buttons in Live › Buttons (V9 p20–24).

  • Page menu › Add Steps scene for each look, in cue-list order.
  • Page menu › Solo buttons, so pressing one look turns the last one off. Otherwise scenes stack.
  • Button menu › Button trigger › Keyboard gives each one a key.
  • Button menu › Flash button lights it only while held. Good for a strobe the operator hits on the music.
  • Live settings › Fade in/out time between scene buttons sets a crossfade between them (V9 p22).
  • Page menu › Add TimeLine puts a whole timeline on one button (V9 p20).

4. Check it Everyone

  1. Unplug the DMX adapter. The 3D view only drew for us with no adapter connected.
  2. Windows › 3DView. Right-click it › Fixtures setting to place each light where it hangs (V9 p32).
  3. In Live, top right, the cube is 3D output and the icon beside it is DMX. A green line under one means it's on.
  4. In Editor › Steps, turn the DMX and 3D buttons off. The Editor overrides Live, so with them on the timeline seems to do nothing.
  5. Play it. Windows › DMX Bargraph shows the actual numbers going out, if the 3D view is confusing (V9 p4).
  6. Record the 3D view for the performer: Cmd + Shift + 5 › Record Selected Portion. ShowXpress has no video export, and the Mac recorder doesn't capture the sound, so add the song afterwards.
  7. At the venue: plug the adapter in, make sure DMX has its green line, play.

When it's wrong Everyone

What you seeWhat it was for us
Music plays, lights don't.3D output off, or the Editor's DMX/3D buttons on (they override Live).
Editor › Fixtures is an empty grid.The show is in the wrong folder. It must be under ~/TheLightingController/LightShows.
Your timeline isn't in the Open list.A different show is open (check the title bar), or the files are in the wrong folder.
Fades slam on.The step duration is far shorter than meant. It's in hundredths: 00:02.00 is 2 s.
A look keeps re-fading.The scene looped. Make its last step long.
Fast flashes drift off the beat, or one goes missing.Step lengths not on the 0.05 s tick, or a block join in the middle of a run.
Lights blink off for an instant.A block join at a bad moment. Move it into a held look.
A light strobes or does something odd on its own.A non-zero shutter or mode channel switched on the fixture's built-in effects.
Files you edited outside ShowXpress went back to the old version.Quit ShowXpress before changing files by hand. My best guess is it saves over them when it closes.

For programmers: generate the files Programmers

Typing dozens of scenes is slow and error-prone. Everything ShowXpress reads is plain text, so for a long piece a script is faster. Quit ShowXpress before writing. A show folder:

~/TheLightingController/LightShows/<show>/
  fixtures.ini                    the patch: [FixtureN] address, name, model, id
  fixtures/<model>.txt            fixture profile: channel order and ranges
  scenes/*.scex                   one XML file per scene
  Live/TimeLine/timelines/*.tml   ini, one [Event_N] per block
  Live/music.wav                  the song
  3DView/fixtures.ini             where each light sits in the 3D view

A scene

<Scene>
  <Fixtures>
    <Fixture id="1685573658" name="CH1: SlimPAR 56 (7CH)" model="SlimPAR 56 (7CH)"/>
  </Fixtures>
  <Steps>
    <Step length="200">
      <Fixture id="1685573658">
        <Channel index="0" value="255" fade="1"/>
        …
        <Channel index="6" value="255" fade="1"/>
      </Fixture>
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Scene>
  • Fixture ids come from fixtures.ini. Channel indexes follow the profile's channel order (ours: 0 red … 6 dimmer).
  • length is hundredths of a second. Playback rounds each step up to 0.05 s, so keep lengths multiples of 5.
  • fade="1" fades across the whole step; 0 snaps.

A timeline

[Params]
LightTimeLines = 1
MediaTimeLines = 1
ShowWaveForm = 1
MaxTime = 0:04:00
TimeLine_1 = V I D E O   P I C T U R E   T I M E L I N E
TimeLine_2 = music
TimeLine_3 = lights
[Event_0]
TimeLineIndex = 2
StartTime = 0:00:00.00
Path = Live/music.wav
Length = 0:03:52.00
[Event_1]
TimeLineIndex = 3
StartTime = 0:00:04.30
Path = 001 at 4.3s.scex
Length = 0:00:05.20
Speed = 100
SpeedType = 2

Path is just the scene's file name, looked up in scenes/. Start times work in tenths.

Or reuse this site's generator

showxpress.js in this folder already does all of this from a cue plan: blocks joined at safe moments, steps on the 0.05 s tick, a 3D layout and an install script, tested against the plan across the whole song (test/showxpress.test.mjs). To use it for another piece, write their cue list as a plan in this app's cue format (README.md, and normalizeCue in cues.js), put the venue's files in venue/, and update the light list (GROUPS in cues.js, SHAPES in app.js) and the channel order (SLIMPAR in showxpress.js). It's built for these 12 SlimPARs today, so a different rig needs those changes first.

The download includes a "Timing test" timeline: 40 steps of 0.25 s, then 40 of 0.07 s. Run it after any ShowXpress update. If the second half takes 2.8 s instead of about 4 s, the tick has changed.
