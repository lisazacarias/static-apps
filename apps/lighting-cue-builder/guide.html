How to light a piece with this

Everything we learned lighting the Teen Titans showcase, written so you can help someone else with theirs. The first half needs no programming. The second half is for whoever changes the code.

What it is

The page plays the song and draws the stage, so you can see what every light is doing at any moment. The plan is a list of cues: "at 0:27.54, lights 1 to 12 fade to warm white over 2 seconds."

From that one plan it makes everything the venue might want:

  • a video of the stage with the song, to show the dancers or the tech;
  • a spreadsheet, one row per moment and one coloured column per light;
  • a cue sheet, plain text to paste into a submission form;
  • a ShowXpress download that plays the plan on real lights (see the ShowXpress guide).

The published page is read-only. It always shows the plan that was last pushed. Add ?edit to the address to get the editor.

Words you'll need Everyone

Light (or fixture)
One lamp. Each has a number on the stage map. Here: 3 and 7 are on the poles, 10 on the loops, 11 is the backdrop, the rest are the floor.
Cue
One change at one moment: which lights, what colour, how bright, and how they get there.
Look
What the whole stage looks like between changes. The Looks view shows each one as a little stage picture.
Snap and fade
A snap changes instantly. A fade takes time (2 seconds by default). A fade from dark comes up in the new colour; a fade on a lit light blends colours, which is a crossfade.
Level
Brightness, 1 to 100%. To dim a light that's already on, cue it again lower.
All others off
Takes every light the cue doesn't name dark. With no lights named, it's a blackout.
Flash run
One cue that fires again and again on the beat: hits, pulses, strobes, chaos.
Section
A label for where each song in the mix starts. Purely for finding your way.
DMX, patch, address
DMX is the cable signal lights listen to. The patch is the venue's list of which light listens at which address (here 1, 33, 65 …). You get it from the venue tech; you don't choose it.
Controller
The program or desk that sends DMX. We used ShowXpress on a Mac. A venue might use a different one.

Step by step Everyone

1. Talk to the venue tech first

The answers decide what you can hand them. Ask early; it took us a few rounds.

  • Who runs the lights on the night: you from a laptop, or the tech? Does the show run from a timeline that follows the music, or does someone press GO for each cue?
  • What controller do they use? If it's ShowXpress, ask for the show's fixtures.ini and the fixture file (ours was SlimPAR 56 (7CH).txt). Those are what make the download match their lights.
  • Which lights are which on their stage map? Is there a light in the patch that isn't on the map? (Ours had a 13th we never placed, so the show leaves it alone.)
  • Are strobes or fast flashing allowed, and do they need a warning posted?
  • What format do they want: cue sheet, spreadsheet, video, or show files?

2. Get the music and mark sections

Use the exact file the show will play. Times only line up with that file. Mark where each song in the mix starts (+ Section at playhead, or Paste a list… with one 1:23.4 Name per line).

3. Find the moments

Times are to the hundredth of a second. A hit at 0:57.66 that you light at 0:57.9 looks late.

  • Arrow keys move 0.1 s (Shift for 1 s). Loop here (L) repeats a few seconds so you can hear one hit over and over.
  • The zoom strip shows 8 seconds of waveform around the playhead. Drums show as spikes.
  • For hits on a beat, make a flash run and use Tap (T) while it plays. It works out the tempo and the first hit.
  • If a passage repeats, find it once and say "same as 0:36.79". That's how we got Raven's drum fill exactly.

4. Write down what you want, in words

Per section: who's the focus, what colours, snap or fade, how busy. Then turn it into cues, either by asking Claude or in the editor.

5. Check it

  • Play it start to finish on the page. Then Looks: one picture per change makes repeats and mistakes jump out.
  • Download video and watch it with the dancer. People notice things in a video they don't on the page.
  • Check on the real controller too. The page was right and ShowXpress was off more than once (see Lessons).

6. Hand it over

Spreadsheet or cue sheet for a tech who'll program it themselves. Video so they see the intent. ShowXpress download if it'll run on ShowXpress; the ShowXpress guide covers installing and playing it.

Asking Claude for changes Everyone

Most of this show was built by describing changes to Claude in a session on this repo. Claude edits starter-plan.json, runs the tests, pushes, and merges when you say so. What made requests land first time:

  • Times, to the hundredth, and light numbers.
  • Snap or fade, and which direction. "Fade" got read as a crossfade when fade-out was meant. Say "snap on, fade out over 1 s".
  • Counts the way a dancer says them. "1 and 2&" is clear. Check you said the pattern you meant; we lost a round on that.
  • Where it stops. "Chaos from 3:17 to 3:22, then normal, then chaos again from 3:40 to the end."
  • For a glitch: the time and the light. "Light 4 blinks off around 2:14 in ShowXpress but not on the page." A screen recording helps a lot.

Making cues yourself Everyone

Open the page with ?edit on the end. Your edits save in your browser only, not on the published page.

  1. Load the song (or it loads the show's). Play to a moment and press Add cue here (C).
  2. Pick lights by clicking them on the stage, or with Apparatus / Floor / Backdrop / All.
  3. Choose On or Off, colour, level, Snap or Fade. Add an End if the lights should go out by themselves.
  4. For hits: tick Flash on the beat, set the beat and count, then Tap along. Soft pulse sets up a gentle breathe.
  5. Save cue. The stage previews while you edit.

Two cues at the same moment: the one entered later wins.

To share your work: Export JSON and send the file. Someone with repo access (or Claude) replaces starter-plan.json with it, and the published page updates. Copy preview link is a quicker read-only snapshot that lives in the link itself.

A warning shows when a run flashes more than 3 times a second, the usual photosensitivity line (WCAG 2.3.1 is the web version). Stage rules vary; ask the venue.

Looks that worked Everyone

Taste, not rules. These are what the dancers liked after seeing videos.

  • Solos: the soloist's apparatus in warm white at full, the other apparatus dark, everyone else at about 55% in soft colours.
  • Keep the apparatus brightest in busy parts. In the strobes the active pole or loops stays full and the floor flashes at 50%, so the dancer doesn't get lost.
  • Sharp switches for punchy intros; fade-outs for hits (snap on, ease away). Crossfades for calm passages.
  • Breathing: lights pulsing up and down, each slightly out of step and in a random order, not going round in a circle.
  • Chaos: every light on its own shuffled run of the palette. Use it in short bursts; it stops meaning anything if it runs long.
  • Sweeps: a colour change rippling across the stage once on a big moment. Once lands; repeated, it gets busy.
  • Pick a palette (ours was the five Teen Titans logo colours) and stay in it.

Lessons Everyone

  • The page and the controller can disagree. ShowXpress plays steps in 0.05 s ticks and rounded our timing, so strobes drifted off the beat. We measured it with a timing test and now line everything up to its ticks. Always check on the real thing.
  • Make changes on the page, never by hand in the controller. The next install replaces whatever was changed in ShowXpress.
  • Quit ShowXpress before installing. It saves over the new files when it closes.
  • Shows live in the home folder, ~/TheLightingController/LightShows, not next to the app.
  • The 3D view only draws with the DMX adapter unplugged, and the Editor's output overrides Live.
  • Watch the video with the dancer early. Every big change of direction came from them seeing it, not reading it.
  • Keep the song file fixed. A re-export with a different start shifts every cue.

How it's built Programmers

Plain HTML, CSS and ES modules, no build step, served from GitHub Pages under a subpath (so every path is relative). Run python3 -m http.server 8000 from the repo root and open /apps/lighting-cue-builder/?edit.

A cue

{
  "id": "example-strobe", "time": 150.2, "end": null, "outFade": null,
  "lights": [7], "action": "on", "color": "#ffffff", "level": 100,
  "transition": "sharp", "fade": 0.16, "othersOff": false, "note": "",
  "repeat": {
    "bpm": 165, "beats": 0.25, "count": 16, "onFrac": 0.5,
    "altColor": null, "dimTo": 0, "keepColor": false,
    "stagger": { "3": 0.1 },
    "cycle": ["#ec1b24", "#00acee"]
  },
  "option": null
}
  • end takes the lights out with the same transition, or over outFade seconds if set (snap on, fade away).
  • repeat makes a flash run: count flashes every beats at bpm, each lit for onFrac of the gap, dropping to dimTo% between. altColor alternates two colours; cycle steps through a list instead; stagger delays individual lights (seconds) so one cue can ripple; keepColor changes brightness only.
  • option puts a cue in a named alternative (a plan and a backup); optionPicks on the plan picks one.
  • Later cues at the same time win. normalizeCue accepts every older shape, so saved plans keep loading. Keep it that way: a storage change needs a normalizer and a test with an old record.

The ShowXpress export

  • The timeline is a row of blocks, each a few seconds long (3 to 8, up to 16), with every change inside a block as a step. One scene per look made lights blink at every join; blocks fixed it.
  • Block edges go on tenths of a second, inside held looks, at least 0.25 s from any snap or short fade. A block opens with a snap to the exact state at that moment.
  • Step lengths are in hundredths of a second, but ShowXpress plays them in 0.05 s ticks, rounding each step up. Steps are placed on that tick grid (TICK) so nothing drifts. The download includes a "Timing test" timeline that proved this.
  • Every step writes all 7 channels of every light: colour on RGB, brightness on the dimmer (SLIMPAR in showxpress.js).
  • test/showxpress.test.mjs plays the generated show back and checks it against stateAt across the whole song.

Setting it up for someone else's show Programmers

Not a programmer? Start a Claude session on this repo and ask it to "set up the lighting cue builder for [show]". Bring: the song, a picture of the venue's stage map, which lights are which, the venue's fixture files if they use ShowXpress, and a palette. Claude does the list below.

For a second show alongside this one, copy the folder to apps/<new-name>/ and change:

  1. Storage keys. Every app on the site shares one origin. Change STORE_KEY, DB_NAME, VIEW_KEY, FOLD_KEY and SHOW_KEY in app.js to a new prefix, or the two shows overwrite each other's saved plans.
  2. The rig in cues.js: GROUPS, APPARATUS, LOGO_COLORS.
  3. The stage map in app.js: SHAPES (an ellipse or rectangle per light, in the diagram's pixel space) and DRAW_ORDER.
  4. The show: a new starter-plan.json (export JSON from the editor works) and show-audio.mp3; SHOW_AUDIO in app.js.
  5. ShowXpress: the venue's files in venue/, then VENUE and SXP_SHOW in app.js. If their fixture isn't a 7-channel RGB + dimmer, change SLIMPAR to its channel order. The install script names the show folder from SXP_SHOW, and the guide's commands need to match.
  6. Tests: the "shipped show plan" test checks Teen Titans moments (the solos, the pole pulsing). Rewrite it for the new show rather than deleting it.
  7. A card in the repo's root index.html, and the copy in showxpress.html and this page.

Venue doesn't use ShowXpress? The spreadsheet, cue sheet and video work anywhere. For another controller, stateAt and changeTimes give you every light's state at every change; a new exporter would sit beside showxpress.js.

Facts about ShowXpress here come from its demo show, its manuals and tests on a real Mac. Other versions may differ; run the timing test after any update.
